# Research protocol — draft, not yet approved

## Working title
**Design and Feasibility Evaluation of a Cloud-Based Multi-Hospital Voluntary Blood Donor Coordination Platform for Sri Lanka**

## Context
Sri Lanka's National Blood Transfusion Service (NBTS) already operates blood-bank and donation systems. This work must **not assume an unmet need**. It asks whether an independently operated coordination platform could add value for voluntary donor recruitment, community donation campaigns or consent-based outreach without duplicating or replacing clinical systems.

## Research questions
1. How do authorized blood-bank staff and volunteer organizers currently coordinate voluntary donors and donation camps?
2. What gaps, if any, remain beyond existing NBTS and local workflows?
3. Which role-based, multi-hospital coordination functions would participants find useful?
4. What data protection, safety, operational and governance controls would be mandatory?
5. Could a serverless architecture support a hypothetical deployment, subject to hosting and data-residency assessments?

## Proposed methods
- Desk review of public NBTS documentation and international public examples.
- Conditional interviews with authorized staff and organizers **only after institutional permission and required ethics clearance or exemption**.
- Optional donor questionnaires **only with appropriate research approval and informed consent**.
- A prototype containing entirely synthetic, non-identifying records.
- Structured usability tasks and a formative analysis of workflow completion, participant feedback and potential operational risks (when approved).

## What this prototype does not establish
- It does not validate clinical blood compatibility, medical eligibility or donation intervals.
- It does not prove safety, security, scalability or regulatory compliance for real donor records.
- It does not demonstrate endorsement or integration from NBTS or any hospital.
- It does not measure lives saved or claim operational benefits before empirical evaluation.

Requirements report, stakeholder workflow model, risk assessment, fictional prototype, usability evaluation where authorized, and research publication/report clearly distinguishing observations from assumptions.
